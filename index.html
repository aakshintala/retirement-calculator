The regime-based Markov model uses historical SPY returns classified into three market regimes based on real returns: bear (≤−5%), flat (−5% to +5%), and bull (≥+5%). It simulates realistic market sequences by modeling regime transitions with a Markov chain—starting from the most recent regime, it probabilistically transitions between regimes based on historical patterns, then samples actual historical years from the selected regime. This preserves realistic bear/bull/flat sequences while allowing combinations not seen exactly in history. The 1980–2024 mode focuses on the modern era with generally lower inflation and different market dynamics.
Bond Tenting Strategy
Maintains a bond reserve equal to the specified years of expenses. During bear/flat market regimes, withdrawals come from bonds first, protecting stock holdings from being sold in down markets. Bonds are refilled conservatively only when critically low. Note: Bond tenting may reduce overall success probability because bonds typically earn lower returns than stocks, but it provides better protection against sequence-of-returns risk during market downturns. Only works with regime-based Markov mode.
